# MASTER PROMPT — PROPERTY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (MULTI-TENANT SAAS)

## 1. Your Role and Project Objective

Act as a senior Laravel developer, software architect, database designer, financial-systems analyst, security engineer, and UI/UX designer.

I want to build a complete, secure, practical, and beginner-friendly **Property Management System** for managing property purchases, sales, investors, expenses, payments, cash/bank accounts, profit/loss, staff, and financial reports.

The system should initially launch with free accounts and support paid subscription plans in the future.

My priority is a reliable, easy-to-use property business management application. Do not turn it into an unnecessarily complicated ERP, banking application, or enterprise accounting system.

Your responsibility is to understand all requirements below, design the database and application properly, and help build a working system with reliable financial calculations, secure tenant isolation, audit history, and a clean interface.

### Important instructions

1. Do not ignore, silently change, or contradict the finalized business requirements below.
2. Do not introduce unnecessary modules or advanced features that are not requested.
3. Use sensible defaults for minor implementation decisions.
4. If a genuinely critical requirement is missing or two requirements conflict, ask a concise clarification before making an irreversible architectural decision.
5. Before writing application code, provide a concise implementation plan, proposed database structure, key relationships, and module sequence.
6. Then implement the project incrementally, ensuring each phase works before moving to the next.
7. Do not provide pseudo-code when working code is required.
8. Do not claim that code, migrations, tests, or features have been implemented or tested unless they actually have.
9. Explain setup and development instructions in simple language suitable for a beginner.
10. If working in an existing project, inspect its files, dependencies, database schema, and current implementation before modifying anything. Preserve working features unless a change is necessary and agreed.
11. When generating code, identify exact file paths and provide complete, usable code or precise patches. Include necessary migrations, models, controllers, routes, Blade views, validation, authorization, and tests.
12. Do not generate the entire application in one enormous, unverified code dump. Build it in manageable, testable phases.

## 3. Multi-Tenant Architecture — Critical Requirement

This is a multi-tenant SaaS-style application.

Each account holder must have one independent workspace.

Each workspace contains its own:
- Properties.
- Investors.
- Staff.
- Purchases and sales.
- Expenses.
- Payments and receipts.
- Cash and bank accounts.
- Investor allocations and distributions.
- Buyer receivables and seller payables.
- Documents.
- Approvals.
- Reports and audit history.

### Strict tenant isolation

An account holder must never access another account holder's private business data.

Enforce tenant isolation in the backend through proper relationships, authorization policies, and scoped queries. Hiding a menu or button is not sufficient.

Check tenant ownership on every relevant request, including:
- Show, edit, update, cancel, and delete/soft-delete operations.
- File upload and download.
- Reports and exports.
- Financial transactions.
- Investor statements.
- Approval requests.
- Search, filters, and AJAX/dependent dropdown endpoints.

Prevent insecure direct object reference (IDOR) vulnerabilities.

All tenant-owned records must be linked to the correct workspace/account holder. Use a consistent tenant ownership strategy throughout the application.

Super Admin must not have silent, unrestricted access to customer business data. Exceptional support access must require authorization, a recorded reason, and an audit trail.

## 4. User Types and Access Control

### A. Super Admin

Super Admin manages the overall platform, including:

- Customer account registrations and activation.
- Account suspension and reactivation.
- Account plans and feature permissions.
- Property and staff limits.
- Document upload/storage entitlements.
- Platform health and backup status.
- Future subscription management.

Super Admin must not silently view or change customer financial records. Exceptional support access must be authorized and logged.

### B. Account Holder

Each account holder manages their own workspace and can:

- Add and manage properties.
- Manage investors.
- Manage property purchases and sales.
- Record expenses, receipts, and payments.
- Manage cash/bank accounts.
- Manage investor allocations and distributions.
- Manage staff and their permissions.
- Configure approval rules.
- View reports and export authorized data.
- Manage reservations, installments, and outstanding balances.
- Manage workspace settings and permitted features.

Account holders must not permanently delete investors or silently erase finalized financial history.

### C. Staff

Account holders can create, update, and deactivate staff accounts.

Provide simple predefined roles, such as Admin and Staff, plus individual permission settings.

Permissions should cover the practical actions needed by the application, such as:
- View.
- Add.
- Edit.
- Cancel.
- Approve, where authorized.
- Export, where authorized.

Use backend authorization for every protected action.

Deactivating a staff member must revoke login access immediately while preserving their historical activity and records.

By default, staff must not approve their own approval requests.

### D. Optional Investor Portal

An account holder may enable investor login for selected investors.

The investor portal is optional and may be restricted by the account's feature entitlement.

An investor may see only their own authorized investments, statements, relevant transactions, and profit distributions.

Investors must not see other investors' private details unless an explicit, justified business rule allows it.

Internal investor management must continue working even when investor portal access is disabled.

## 5. Super Admin Feature and Limits Management

Super Admin must be able to configure each customer account's plan and available features.

The system must support free accounts initially and future paid plans without requiring a major architectural rewrite.

Initially, subscriptions may be managed manually. A payment gateway is not required.

### Configurable account entitlements

Support the following configurable features and limits:

1. Maximum active properties.
2. Maximum staff accounts.
3. Document upload permission.
4. Maximum document storage.
5. Maximum individual file size.
6. Permitted document/file types.
7. Excel/PDF export entitlement, if plan-restricted.
8. Investor portal entitlement.
9. Other future features through a maintainable entitlement mechanism.

The above limits are configurable examples, not predetermined commercial pricing or final numeric limits. Super Admin should be able to set appropriate values.

### Active property counting

Count properties according to the configured definition of active inventory. Sold properties must not count as active inventory.

Do not count cancelled sale records as additional properties.

### Storage restrictions

Document uploads may initially be enabled only for selected pilot accounts, such as one or two accounts, due to storage concerns.

When enabled, authorized users may attach relevant images and PDFs to:
- Purchase agreements.
- Sale agreements.
- Receipts.
- Expenses.
- Payments.
- Other supported business records.

Use private storage, secure downloads, file validation, storage quotas, and role-based authorization.

### Plan changes and suspension

If an account is downgraded or its entitlement is removed:
- Preserve existing business data and documents.
- Do not automatically delete records.
- Apply configured restrictions to future actions where appropriate.
- Inform the account holder when an operation is blocked by a plan limit.

Account suspension must disable access as configured but must not erase financial history.

Design the entitlement mechanism so that new features can be enabled or restricted later.

## 8. Property Purchase and Seller Payments

For each property purchase, track:

- Total purchase price.
- Additional purchase expenses.
- Seller payment history.
- Amount paid.
- Outstanding seller payable balance.
- Payment dates and amounts.
- Payment method and relevant cash/bank account.
- Optional receipt or document attachment when enabled.

Support partial payments and installments without unnecessarily complicated contract-management functionality.

If there are multiple sellers, provide seller-wise payable tracking where needed.

### Critical financial rule

The property purchase price must be counted as property cost only once.

Subsequent payments to the seller settle the outstanding payable and reduce cash/bank balances. They must not add the same purchase cost again.

Purchase expenses must be recorded separately from payments that settle an existing payable.

## 9. Property Expenses and Funding Sources

Support expenses associated with purchasing, owning, maintaining, or selling a property.

Examples include:
- Purchase-related expenses.
- Documentation and transfer charges.
- Repairs and maintenance.
- Agent/broker commission.
- Taxes and government charges.
- Other business or property expenses.

Expense categories should be manageable and extensible without needing a code change for every new category.

### Separate expense recognition from payment

The system must distinguish:
- Expense incurred.
- Amount paid.
- Outstanding payable, if any.

Record relevant details, including amount, date, category, property, payer/funding source, payment method, and status.

An expense may be funded by:
- The account holder.
- One or more investors.
- A combination of the account holder and investors.

Record both:
1. Who actually paid the amount.
2. Whose funds or capital the expense represents.

These concepts must not be confused.

Avoid double-counting an expense when its payable is later paid.

## 10. Sale Management

A sale record should support:

- Property.
- Buyer details.
- Sale agreement date.
- Agreed sale price.
- Advance/token amount.
- Payment schedule, if applicable.
- Sale expenses.
- Amount received.
- Remaining buyer receivable.
- Sale status.
- Finalization date.
- Relevant documents, if enabled.
- Cancellation details, where applicable.

### Sale finalization and profit/loss

When a sale is finalized, calculate actual realized profit/loss using the relevant recorded purchase cost, applicable purchase expenses, and relevant sale expenses.

A simplified conceptual calculation is:

Net realized profit/loss = Sale price − allocated property cost − applicable sale expenses.

The precise cost and expense allocation must follow consistent, documented business rules and must not count the same expense twice.

### Keep profit separate from cash receipts

A sale may be finalized even when the buyer has not paid the entire amount.

Track separately:
- Finalized sale value.
- Calculated realized profit/loss.
- Cash actually received.
- Remaining buyer receivable.
- Investor profit payable.
- Investor profit actually paid.

Do not treat outstanding buyer installments as cash received.

Do not treat an investor profit distribution as a new property expense.

## 13. Multiple Cash and Bank Accounts

The purpose of this module is to track the property's business money. It is not intended to be a complete banking application.

Allow an account holder to create multiple cash accounts and bank accounts.

Each account should track:
- Account name/type.
- Opening balance.
- Receipts.
- Payments/withdrawals.
- Transfers.
- Current balance.

Record relevant transactions with dates, amounts, descriptions, and references to the related property, purchase, sale, expense, or investor transaction where applicable.

### Transfers

Allow money transfers between the account holder's cash/bank accounts.

A transfer must:
- Reduce the source account balance.
- Increase the destination account balance.
- Be recorded as one linked transfer or equivalent balanced entries.
- Not be counted as income or expense.

### Avoid double-counting

Receiving cash from a buyer and later depositing that cash into a bank account must not count as two separate sales or two separate income events.

Likewise, withdrawing money from a bank account into cash must not count as a new expense.

Do not implement bank statement imports, bank reconciliation, or advanced treasury management in the initial version.

## 14. Investor Allocations and Investment Ratios

A property may have multiple investors.

Track:
- Investor.
- Property.
- Investment contribution.
- Contribution date.
- Relevant capital allocation.
- Ownership percentage.
- Profit/loss sharing percentage.
- Investment status and history.

### Allocation cap

Total investor capital allocations must not exceed the property's total purchase cost, defined as purchase price plus purchase-related expenses.

Any remaining amount is treated as the account holder's own investment.

Do not allow allocations to silently exceed this cap. If later changes to property costs affect the calculation, handle them through a validated process.

### Separate ratios

Ownership percentages and profit/loss sharing percentages are separate concepts.

- Ownership percentages default to the investment ratio but may be configured according to the agreement.
- Profit/loss sharing percentages default to the investment ratio but may be configured separately.
- Validate that the relevant active percentages total 100% for the applicable property or allocation group.
- Preserve the applicable percentages and agreement history for finalized transactions.

Do not assume that ownership and profit-sharing ratios are always identical.

Before sale finalization, authorized users may:
- Add an investor.

- Allow an investor to exit.
- Replace an investor.

Record relevant details:
- Effective date.
- Previous and new investor.
- Transferred percentage or capital amount.
- Agreed valuation, where applicable.
- Settlement amount.
- Payment status.
- Reason and audit history.

An investor-to-investor share transfer is not a property sale and must not be recorded as property sale income.

Preserve historical investor allocations and agreements.

After a property sale is finalized, preserve the applicable investor ratios used for that sale. Corrections must use the dedicated audited correction process.

## 16. Investor Profit Distribution

When a property sale is finalized:

1. Calculate total realized profit/loss.
2. Calculate each investor's applicable share according to the recorded profit-sharing percentages.
3. Record the investor's profit payable or loss allocation as applicable.
4. Track actual distributions separately from the calculated payable.

Maintain separate values for:
- Calculated investor profit share.
- Profit payable.
- Amount paid.
- Remaining unpaid amount.
- Payment date and method.
- Related cash/bank account.
- Relevant payment reference.

The account holder decides when to pay investors, subject to the applicable agreements and approval rules.

Available cash and buyer installment receipts must not be confused with calculated profit.

Investor payouts must not be counted again as property expenses.

## 17. Financial Ledger and Integrity

Implement a practical, auditable financial ledger suitable for property business operations and capable of supporting a more formal double-entry accounting system in the future.

Do not build a full enterprise accounting package initially.

Track the necessary financial concepts separately:
- Cash and bank balances.
- Buyer receivables.
- Seller payables.
- Property purchase costs.
- Expenses incurred.
- Expense payments.
- Investor capital.
- Investor profit payable.
- Actual investor distributions.
- Buyer advances and refunds.
- Sale proceeds and realized profit/loss.
- Account-to-account transfers.

Use consistent financial services or calculation classes rather than duplicating calculation logic across controllers and views.

### Financial precision

Use appropriate fixed-precision decimal database columns for PKR amounts, not floating-point values for financial calculations.

Use database transactions for multi-step financial operations.

Use appropriate validation, constraints, and locking strategies where needed to prevent duplicate payments, invalid balances, and inconsistent concurrent updates.

Never calculate a financial report by blindly adding all payment records together without considering the type and accounting meaning of each transaction.

## 18. Corrections, Cancellations, and Audit History

### Draft transactions

Draft transactions may be edited or deleted according to permissions and business rules.

### Confirmed transactions

Confirmed financial transactions must not be silently overwritten or permanently deleted.

Corrections must use a reasoned, audited adjustment process that preserves:
- Original values.
- Corrected values.
- Reason for correction.
- User making the correction.
- Date/time.
- Relevant approval history, where applicable.

If an actual payment is reversed, record a separate reversal entry linked to the original payment.

### Finalized sale corrections

An authorized account holder may correct a finalized sale with a mandatory reason and audit trail. Super Admin approval is not required by default.

Preserve the original sale and correction history. Recalculate applicable balances and profit/loss consistently.

Do not overwrite payments already made to investors. If a correction creates an additional payable or recoverable amount, record it separately.

### Sale cancellation

Sale cancellation must retain the historical sale record.

Record cancellation date/reason, received payments, refunds, outstanding refunds, permitted forfeitures or cancellation charges, and other settlement amounts.

A cancelled sale is not automatically erased from the property's history.
